# UXperts Needed to Design the Future of Debate

- **Type**: Practitioner essay (Timothy High, Canonical Debate Lab, Medium)
- **Source**: https://medium.com/canonical-debate-lab/uxperts-needed-to-design-the-future-of-debate-824e6525ecca
- **Accessed**: 2026-07-11

## Framing

Argues debate/argument-mapping tools face a UX problem categorically harder than most consumer apps: "debate itself is 'a deceptively complex subject'" — a well-designed *simple* interface (à la Google/Twitter's minimalism) risks flattening the structure that makes argument mapping valuable in the first place.

## Nine identified unsolved challenges (selected, most design-relevant)

1. **Ranking vs. full visibility** — rating-sorted columnar views (like Kialo) work well for "which argument wins" but actively conflict with a curator/researcher's need to see "the entire structure of the debate at once." These are different views for different intents, not a single view that serves both.
2. **No single scoring system suffices** — "each has its own inherent biases," and current tools generally lack **roll-up scoring** (how does a leaf-level argument's strength propagate up to affect confidence in the root thesis).
3. **Knowledge-graph integration** — real debates reference external facts/sources; that's a whole extra visualization layer beyond the argument structure itself.
4. **Scale management** — the central open problem: convey "the number of possibilities, give a 10,000-foot view of a subject, and still provide reasonable navigability" as debates grow into the hundreds or thousands of nodes.
5. **Transparency/versioning** — showing how a debate evolved over time, and letting users judge trustworthiness, requires real history tracking, not just a current-state snapshot.
6. **Possibly irreconcilable via one interface** — the essay explicitly concedes: "Perhaps it is not possible to support all these goals simultaneously via a single interface," i.e., multiple purpose-built views/modes may be the honest answer rather than one universal screen.

## Vision statement

The ideal platform stays "an inviting atmosphere that makes it fun to use" while offering "details...perhaps only to those that seek them" — i.e., progressive disclosure again, framed explicitly as the resolution to the simplicity-vs-power tension.

## Relevance

This is the single most directly on-target source in the set: it's a domain expert naming almost exactly the tensions a ComputeNet argumentation-graph frontend will hit (rank vs. full-structure, single vs. roll-up scoring, scale). Its conclusion — multiple purpose-built modes rather than one do-everything screen — is a strong design-guide recommendation, not just an observation.
