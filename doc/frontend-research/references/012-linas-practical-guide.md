# Summary

Frames Fable-era prompting around a central claim: "the model is no longer the bottleneck, you are" — i.e.
the limiting factor shifts from model capability to the human's ability to surface their own hidden
assumptions before delegating. Full technique list is behind a paywall; the free portion outlines structure
and mental models rather than exhaustive detail.

## Structure (as previewed)

- A four-part prompt structure for Fable 5 (parts not fully disclosed in the free preview).
- Five rules for elevating output quality, and three counterproductive habits to actively drop.
- A "map vs. territory" mental model for locating assumption gaps between what was asked and what's
  actually needed.
- An "autonomy playbook" for multi-hour/multi-day sessions that resists drift.
- Before/during/after phase-specific technique sequencing (mirrors the three-phase pattern seen
  independently in `017`).
- A downloadable Claude Skill for a "finding your unknowns" workflow.

## Relevance to this project

Corroborates, from a different angle, the "phase your work" pattern in `017` (explore/plan/execute/verify)
and the "give context/intent, not just steps" theme from `001`, `007`, and `013`. The "map vs. territory"
framing is a useful naming device for the workflow doc: before delegating a UI task to Fable or Opus,
explicitly write down what's assumed vs. what's confirmed about the argumentation-graph data model.
