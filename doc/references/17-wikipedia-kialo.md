# Kialo (Wikipedia)

- **Type**: Encyclopedia overview of a widely-used debate platform
- **Source**: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kialo
- **Accessed**: 2026-07-11

## What it is

Kialo is an online structured-debate platform (founded 2017 by Errikos Pitsos) organizing discussion as a debate tree rather than linear comment threads, covering philosophical, policy, ethical, and scientific topics.

## Interface model

- **Tree structure** — a central thesis at the root, with Pro and Con columns branching beneath it, recursively (arguments can have their own sub-pro/sub-con arguments).
- **Rating system** — registered users rate each argument's impact on its parent claim; arguments are sorted by average rating, so the most-impactful counterpoints surface without manual curation.
- **Navigation** — branches can be collapsed/expanded; a sunburst diagram gives an overview of the whole debate's topology; users can jump in at any node.
- **Perspectives** — impact ratings can be filtered/viewed by stakeholder group (e.g., supporters vs. opponents vs. moderators) rather than only a single aggregate score.
- **Guided voting** — a walkthrough mode steps a user through key branches to arrive at an overall stance on the thesis.

## Documented criticisms

- Structural rigidity: one study noted Kialo "has simplified its notion of argument structure so much that there is very little flexibility left" — users can agree/disagree with a side but can't easily register a nuanced or conditional position.
- Moderator gatekeeping: submitted arguments "have to get past the gatekeepers," which can exclude legitimate but unpopular-with-moderators perspectives.

## Relevance

Kialo is the most mature production example of "overview first" applied to argument graphs (sunburst overview + drill-down tree), and its rating-based auto-sort is a concrete, working answer to the "how do I decide which of 40 rebuttals to show by default" problem this whole reference set keeps returning to. Its criticisms are equally useful: rigid pro/con-only structure loses nuance, and centralized moderation creates a trust bottleneck — both are relevant constraints to weigh against Kialo's UX wins.
