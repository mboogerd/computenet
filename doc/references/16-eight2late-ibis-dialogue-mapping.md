# IBIS, Dialogue Mapping, and the Art of Collaborative Knowledge Creation

- **Type**: Practitioner blog essay (Eight to Late)
- **Source**: https://eight2late.com/2009/07/23/ibis-dialogue-mapping-and-the-art-of-collaborative-knowledge-creation/
- **Accessed**: 2026-07-11

## Minimal notation, maximal use

> "The IBIS notation consists of just *three* elements (issues, ideas and arguments) that can be connected in a small number of ways."

Reinforces (from a different author) the same point as Conklin (15): the notation's power comes from restraint, not from richness of vocabulary.

## Facilitation as craft, not just process

Drawing an analogy to Piet Mondrian's stylistic evolution, the piece argues facilitation quality is closer to an artistic skill than a mechanical process — "using tools and methods with groups is a matter of how effective, artistic, creative, etc. whoever is applying and organizing the approach can be with the situation, constraints, and people." Six named competencies: paying attention, personalizing, imaging (metaphor/story), serious play, collaborative enquiry, and crafting (synthesizing into a coherent whole).

## The map as medium

> The map provides "the canvas, brush and paint to express knowledge as art," capturing and relating "snippets of knowledge...over time," letting a group grasp "the discussion as a whole" — while acknowledging that managing complexity in a *long* discussion remains genuinely hard, not automatically solved by the notation.

## Relevance

A useful counterweight to a purely "graph visualization best practices" framing: for argumentation specifically, the tool's job includes supporting a live facilitator's judgment calls (what to capture, how to phrase it, when to merge duplicate points), not just rendering a static structure well. A frontend should leave room for **light-touch moderation/curation actions** (merge, rephrase, flag-as-duplicate) as first-class, fast interactions — not just viewing.
